Why it exists

When it rains, laundry stops.

Damp British winters and the Indian monsoon push wet clothes indoors. Airers are slow, radiators are costly and tumble dryers stay where they are plugged in.

of surveyed Indian women dry clothes indoors during the monsoon.

Features

Small on the outside. Smart on the inside.

Heated, high-speed airflow

Built on the airflow expertise Dyson developed for hair care, so air moves through fabric instead of sitting on top of it.

A frame that blooms

One press opens a light petal frame with room for a small load, then it folds flat again.

Moisture sensing

Reads how damp your washing still is and tells you when it is done.

Rain-aware app

A companion app that can nudge you when rain is forecast and track every cycle.

Pocket to bag

Closed, it is a palm-sized disc. Slip it into a bag, a gym kit or a suitcase.

Plug in or charge first

Dry while it charges for small loads, or charge it up and dry anywhere on battery.

Designed to be certified

Heating and moisture sensing are planned to go through safety certification before any launch.

Questions

Good to know

A 99 Wh battery can evaporate roughly 100 g of water, enough for one or two light items. For a full small load, plug it in: about 75 minutes at 300 W.

There is no price yet. A premium, Dyson-style price would be set only after testing what people would pay.

Safety is the first thing a real product would have to prove. The heating element and moisture sensing would be certified before launch.

UK cities first, aimed at young renters. India’s monsoon cities follow in a second phase.
